# Style guide - modern Icelandic for Preiss Workshop and Studio Esja

Written 2026-09-19 from Tenis's own sent mail (2023-2026), the Icelandic his
clients and suppliers write to him, the website's CRM mail templates, and
the reference sources logged in `sources.md`. Labels: **[V]** verified
against a reference source (search-result excerpt; the pages themselves
could not be opened from the sandbox), **[C]** corpus evidence (what real
people wrote in this mailbox), **[K]** linguistic knowledge not re-verified
this session, **[A]** house decision.

## 1. Register and address

- **þú, always.** Formal address (þéringar: þér, yður, yðar, plural verb
  with one person) disappeared from spoken Icelandic in the latter half of
  the 20th century [V - Vísindavefurinn 31816]. In a 2026 business mail it
  reads as parody or as machine translation. Careful: `þér` is also the
  ordinary dative of þú ("gaman að heyra frá þér") - that is correct and
  common [C]. What is wrong is `þér` with a plural verb ("Getið þér ...").
- **First name, nominative.** "Sæll Jón," "Sæl Anna," [C]. Icelanders
  address each other by first name in every register; surnames are for
  invoices and kennitala lines. Nominative after Sæll/Sæl/Kæri/Kæra [V -
  ávarpsfall entry: nominative is used for address in Icelandic].
- **Singular vs plural you.** One person: þú/þig/þér/þín. A couple, a
  company, a design studio: þið/ykkur/ykkar. Clients write "þið" to the
  workshop as a matter of course ("Takið þið að ykkur ...?") [C]. Tenis
  answers as "ég" for himself and "við" for the workshop; both are fine in
  one mail [C]. The employee writing on the workshop's behalf uses "við"
  and never "ég" [A].
- **Gender agreement** on adjectives and participles follows the recipient:
  ánægður/ánægð, búinn/búin, velkominn/velkomin; neuter plural for a mixed
  group or a company (velkomin, sæl öll) [K].

## 2. Greetings (ávarp)

| Situation | Write | Why |
|---|---|---|
| First reply, gender known | Sæll Jón, / Sæl Anna, | Standard, slightly formal, exactly what Tenis and his clients use [C] |
| Client opened with Góðan dag / Góðan daginn | Góðan dag Anna, or Sæl Anna, | Mirror the client's level [C] |
| Client opened with Hæ / Hæhæ, or long-standing client | Hæ Anna, | Mirror; "Hæ" is now used even by banks and utilities as a neutral opener [V - excerpt attributed to Eiríkur Rögnvaldsson's language column] |
| Gender unknown, shared inbox, generated mail | Góðan dag, | Never "Sæl/l" - a slash greeting is a mail-merge tell [A, V-excerpt] |
| A couple or two named people | Sæl bæði, / Sæl Anna og Jón, | [K] |
| A group, a company team | Sæl öll, / Góðan dag, | [K] |
| Second and later replies in one thread | greeting optional; "Sæl," or none | Tenis does this [C] |

Comma after the greeting, then a blank line. "Sæll," alone (no name) is
fine in a fast thread but a little cool in a first reply [C].

Name gender in a hurry: -son -> Sæll; -dóttir -> Sæl; -bur or unknown ->
Góðan dag. Foreign names -> Góðan dag or English.

## 3. Closings (kveðja)

| Situation | Write |
|---|---|
| First contact, an offer, anything a client may forward | Bestu kveðjur, / Með kveðju, |
| Warm ongoing relationship | Kær kveðja, (very common from suppliers and designers [C]) |
| Active thread, quick reply | Kveðja, / Kv, Tenis |
| Chat-speed reply inside a thread | Kkv, Tenis (Tenis's habit [C]) - never in a first reply or an offer |
| Thanks-only reply | Takk kærlega! / Takk, Tenis |

Advice from Icelandic writing guides (via search excerpts): the
abbreviations Kkv, Mbk, Kv, Mkv are considered insufficient for a formal
message; "Með kveðju", "Bestu kveðjur", "Kær kveðja", "Góðar kveðjur" are
the recommended forms; "Með kærri kveðju" is old-fashioned [V - DV 2011,
gerumbetur.ritmal.is]. Case: "Bestu kveðjur" (nominative plural) and "Með
kveðju" (dative after með) - never "Bestu kveðju" [K].

Do not translate "Best regards" or "Kind regards" literally; "Vinsamlegar
kveðjur" is not a thing [K].

## 5. Shape of a mail

- **Subject in the client's language.** Icelandic subjects in the corpus:
  "Tilboð í hillur og skápa", "Tilboð í filmun", "Fyrirspurn um smíði á
  rúmgafli", "Sérsmíði og verkefnastjórn", "Filmun á skáp og eldhússtólar"
  [C]. Pattern: `Tilboð í <verk>` (accusative), `Fyrirspurn um <hlut>`,
  `Staðfesting: <hvað>`, `Reikningur nr. <n>`. Keep "Re:" when replying.
- **Open with the thing** ("Takk fyrir upplýsingarnar og myndirnar." then
  the price) - Tenis never writes a warm-up paragraph [C].
- **One idea per paragraph**, 1-3 sentences each. Prices on their own
  lines. Bullets for two or more totals [C: "Heildarverð verður því: •
  {X} kr. með VSK (miðað við lægra efnisverð) • ..."].
- **End with the next step** (send photos; which day suits; call me) and a
  closing. No "Ég vona að þetta hjálpi" filler.
- **Length:** most of Tenis's Icelandic mails are 40-120 words [C]. Longer
  only for a real explanation (the IKEA comparison mail, ~120 words).

## 6. Sentence style

- Short declaratives; verbs early; concrete nouns (hnotuspónn, 16 mm MDF,
  RAL 1021, 2.008 mm). This is the brand guide's "precise people" test in
  Icelandic.
- Use the everyday craft words, not dictionary formalities: "filma"
  (verb), "kíkja á" (take a look), "mæla" (measure), "græja" (sort out,
  colloquial - fine in chat, not in an offer), "koma á staðinn" [C].
- Conditional subjunctive is the polite Icelandic engine: "væri", "gæti",
  "myndi", "ef þú gætir sent..." [C]. It softens without hedging.
- "Endilega" = "please do / feel free" (Endilega sendið teikningarnar...)
  [C]. "Vinsamlegast" is stiffer; keep it for instructions on documents.
- Questions are fine and human: "Hvaða lit ert þú að hugsa þér?" "Hentar
  þér miðvikudagur?" [C].
- No exclamation marks in offers [brand guide]; at most one in a warm
  reply ("Takk kærlega!") [C]. No "!!!". Emoji only when the client used
  one first, one at most, never in an offer [C, A].

## 8. A tilboð in Icelandic (structure)

Maps the workshop brand guide's offer structure. Headings that Icelandic
clients expect on a quote:

    Tilboð nr. Q-2026-00XX · dags. 18. september 2026 · gildir til 2. október 2026
    Verkið                    - what will be built, specifically
    Efni og smíði             - materials and construction, briefly why
    Verð                      - Efni / Vinna / Samtals án vsk. / Vsk. 24% / Samtals með vsk.
    Innifalið                 - included
    Ekki innifalið            - excluded, plainly (rafmagnsvinna - löggiltur rafvirki; múrverk; málun)
    Afgreiðslutími            - "um 3 vikur frá samþykki"
    Greiðsluskilmálar         - terms as the quote calculation states them
    Það sem getur breytt verðinu - what would change the price
    Samþykki                  - signature / accept line

Vocabulary: "fast verð" (fixed), "gróf áætlun / verðhugmynd" (rough),
"sundurliðun" (breakdown), "innborgun" (deposit), "eftirstöðvar"
(balance), "afhending" (delivery), "uppsetning" (installation),
"frágangur" (finishing). Exclusions are respect: state them.

## 9. Brand voices

**Preiss Workshop.** Calm competence. The client should feel measured
twice. Say what will be built, from what, by when, for how much, and what
is not included. Value framing when challenged: fixed price vs. hourly;
compare to the real alternative ("ný sérsmíðuð innrétting ... kosta
talsvert meira"); offer a simpler variant instead of a discount [C].
Never "lausnir", "hágæða" as a filler, "einstakt tækifæri", "ótrúlegt".

**Studio Esja.** Calm, serious, human. The making is visible: steinleir,
handrennt, glerungur, brennsla, Álafoss. Sensory words earn their place
(mattur, grábrúnn, fíngerð tilbrigði, þung í hendi) and stop before
poetry. Variation is stated as a fact of the craft, not an apology
("enginn bolli er nákvæmlega eins og annar"). Japanese terms stay as they
are (yunomi, wabi-sabi) - do not translate or explain them at length.
Never: "lúxus", "einstakt tækifæri", "takmarkað upplag!!!", sale-speak,
or claims about durability or dishwasher safety that were not confirmed.

## 10. Reviewing someone else's Icelandic

Read as the recipient first, grammarian second. Rank findings by what a
client would notice: greeting and closing, mixed language, machine-
translation idioms, then numbers and dates, then case and agreement,
then style. Give every finding as wrong -> right with a one-line reason.
Never rewrite a whole text that works; fix what is wrong and say why.
