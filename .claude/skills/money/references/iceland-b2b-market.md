# Selling to businesses in Iceland: who the buyers are, how they must buy, and how to get paid

Built 2026-09-20 from main-pc, from Statistics Iceland, the companies' own
sites, Althingi's statute texts, island.is regulations and Creditinfo. Every
quotation was seen in the page text; the `isb-*` claims in `claims.json`
re-check the ones a script can reach. Facts only. The setting: a small workshop
selling interior film work, signage or fit-out to hotels, landlords and housing
companies in the capital region, and needing its invoices paid on time. Contact
details below are the ones each company publishes itself; none publishes a
purchasing or maintenance buyer by name. This file names no customer of his and

## 8. What the facts allow [I]

- The capital region's hotel market is about sixty houses, and four groups run
  a large share of the rooms. A short named list covers it; this is an account
  list, not an advertising market.
- Landlords publish maintenance addresses (Eik, Kaldalón), which is the door
  for refurbishment work between tenants. Hotel groups publish only general
  addresses, so the first step there is finding the person by phone.
- Félagsbústaðir and the student housing bodies are public or near-public
  buyers: small jobs fall under the compare-several-firms rule, not a tender.
- Two firms already sell interior film by name. The market is proven and not
  empty; nobody was found selling it to hotels as a refurbishment programme.
- A quote that names a due date, the statutory interest, the 6,700-króna
  compensation and a reservation of the right to rescind uses rights the law
  already gives; without a due date, interest starts a month later.
- The bankruptcy challenge is the strongest lawful lever against a company
  that can pay and does not, and it costs the creditor if the debtor is empty.
  It is a lawyer's step, checked with one before use.
