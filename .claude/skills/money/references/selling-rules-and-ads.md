# Selling a start-a-business course honestly: earnings claims, testimonials, refunds, ads and e-mail

Built 2026-09-20 from main-pc, from the rule texts themselves: the US Code of
Federal Regulations (eCFR, current to 2026-09-17) and FTC pages, the EU
directives in their consolidated versions on EUR-Lex, Iceland's statutes on
Althingi's site (text of 1 September 2026), Neytendastofa, the UK CAP Code, and
the advertising policies of Meta, Google and TikTok. Every quotation was seen
in the page text; the `ads-*` claims in `claims.json` re-check the ones a
script can reach. Facts only. The setting: a tradesman in Iceland selling an
online course to private buyers worldwide, in English, promising a skill and
the start of a small business. This is a map for a lawyer's review of the sales
page, not a substitute for it.

## 10. What the facts allow [I]

- A course that teaches a trade and how to find customers is not a US
  business opportunity. It becomes one the moment the seller promises to
  supply customers, leads, accounts or to buy back work. A job board or
  referral promise inside the programme needs a lawyer's eye first.
- Any income figure on the sales page needs written proof in hand, and one
  student's result will be read as typical unless typical results are shown.
  The safest sales page sells the skill and shows real jobs with their real
  prices, and says what most students achieve once that is known.
- For EU and Icelandic buyers the checkout needs the consent tick, the
  acknowledgement and the confirmation e-mail, or the buyer may have the
  course and the money back for up to a year. Live coaching stays refundable
  for 14 days whatever the tick says, until fully delivered.
- Countdown timers must be true, discounts must refer to a real 30-day price,
  testimonials must be real, unbought and documented.
- Ads that speak to the viewer's money trouble or promise income are refused
  by Meta and Google before any regulator sees them. Ads that show the work
  pass.
- Selling in English to buyers abroad is outside the Icelandic-language rule;
  ads aimed at Iceland must be in Icelandic.
- A mailing list built by opt-in on his own site meets both e-mail rules;
  a bought or scraped list meets neither.
