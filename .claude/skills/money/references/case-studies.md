# Case studies: solo and bootstrapped software businesses, with the base rates

Researched 2026-09-19. The sandbox could open no founder page (levels.io,
microconf.com, bannerbear.com, nathanbarry.com, plausible.io, acquire.com
and the rest all returned 403), so every number here is **[S]** snippet
(search-result text attributed to the URL) and, where it is a founder's own
figure, **[SR]** self-reported; **[I]** marks inference; **[G]** gaps.
Founder revenue is marketing until audited; none of it is. Secondary
"founder story" sites (starterstory, supabird, getlatka) are pointers, not
evidence; where only they carry a number it is marked low confidence.

Contents: 1 the cases · 2 time to a living wage · 3 base rates · 4 exits ·
5 the patterns · 6 sources

## 2. Time to a living wage (roughly $5-10k a month), from the dated snippets [I]

| Case | Start → living wage | What made it possible |
|---|---|---|
| FeedbackPanda | ~6 months to $10k MRR | the customer was in the house; community distribution |
| Bannerbear | ~11 months to ramen ($3.8k), ~15 months to $10k MRR | after 7 dead launches; 50/50 code-marketing |
| ConvertKit | ~26 months to $5k MRR | only after niche + direct sales + concierge |
| Storemapper | $1k/month side project → $21k/month by 2016; 5 years to sale | freelancing paid the bills; Shopify ecosystem |
| Plausible | 324 days to $400 MRR; ~2 years to $250k ARR | two founders, content, patience |
| TypingMind, ShipFast, Wathan's book | days to five figures | a pre-existing audience - the exception that proves the rule |

Nobody in the set started from zero income and zero audience and reached a
living wage inside six months except FeedbackPanda, which had an embedded
customer. Plan on 12-24 months of ramp with sold hours underneath.

Read [I]: the MicroConf sample is founders who already have a SaaS and
came to MicroConf; the population of all attempts is worse. A quarter of
survivors are under $1k MRR; a living wage is a minority outcome; the
founders who got there mostly did so on their third-plus attempt.

## 4. Exits

- Acquire.com, January 2026 report: "The standard range across deal sizes
  sits between 3 and 5x net income, with outliers above that for
  exceptionally clean businesses."; "nearly all acquisitions closing on
  Acquire.com today come from profitable businesses"; multiples "dropping
  from a peak of ~17x down to around 5.5x net income" after the 2022 rate
  rise (segment unclear) [S: blog.acquire.com].
- FeedbackPanda "seven figures" on ~$660k ARR ≈ 1.5-3× ARR; Small Bets
  ~7-8× profit from a strategic buyer [I].
- Exit math [I]: a $5-10k a month profit business is a $200-600k asset.
  Useful, not retirement; the FI number in `fi-and-debt.md` is the target,
  and a sale is one way to fund it.

## 5. The patterns (all [I], drawn from snippet-level cases)

1. **Low hit rates are normal even for the winners.** Several cheap
   attempts, each small enough that failure costs weeks; the first two or
   three are expected to die.
2. **Income was bridged, never gambled.** Freelancing (Tringas), a salary
   until a launch replaced it (Wathan, Dinh), a customer in the house
   (Kahl), ramen profitability as an explicit checkpoint (Yongfook). The
   engineer who quits to build on savings appears only in the failed
   batches (Yongfook 2019, Levels' 66+ dead projects).
3. **Distribution was chosen with or before the product**: a platform
   ecosystem with built-in discovery (Storemapper on Shopify; Walling's
   add-ons), the customer's own community (FeedbackPanda), a built
   audience (Dinh, Lou, Levels, Wathan, Vassallo), content and open source
   (Plausible, Bannerbear), direct sales with concierge onboarding
   (ConvertKit). Build-first-and-hope appears in none of the successes.
4. **Recurring B2B revenue built the sellable assets** (Storemapper,
   FeedbackPanda, Bannerbear, Plausible, ConvertKit, Sidekiq). One-time
   products produced the fastest cash but needed an audience and decayed
   (ShipFast) or migrated to subscriptions (TypingMind's Team plan).
5. **Narrow niches moved fastest**: store locators for shops, feedback for
   ESL teachers, OG images for developers, analytics for privacy-minded
   site owners, email for professional bloggers - one identifiable group,
   one repetitive pain, low monthly prices.
6. **For Tenis** [I]: keep billable work as the bridge; build a Walling
   Step-1 add-on or a narrow B2B tool for the trade he is embedded in
   (CNC shops, small workshops - the FeedbackPanda position without a
   social audience); pick the channel with built-in discovery before
   writing code (Dynomotion's user base for HelmCNC, a marketplace or
   practitioner community for the shop tool); treat the first attempts as
   experiments with a budget in hours, not a bet with the mortgage.
