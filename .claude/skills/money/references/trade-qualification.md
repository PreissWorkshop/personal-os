## 4. What the facts allow [I]

- For New Zealand a formal certificate is not the gate for a work visa (two
  years of evidenced experience is enough) but it is the gate for the faster
  trades pathway to residence; without one, residence runs through the
  experience pathway, which needs two years of skilled work in New Zealand.
- The Icelandic route to a certificate is raunfærnimat in carpentry, then the
  courses that remain (taught in Icelandic), then the exam, held at least
  yearly. Furniture making has an exam but no listed raunfærnimat.
- Questions for IÐAN's counsellor (the first talk is part of the process):
  can the assessment be done in English; how many credits does carpentry
  experience usually cover; what remains and how long it takes; does
  raunfærnimat alone admit to the exam.
- The protected-trade rules in section 2 describe the law as written. How they
  apply to a workshop that makes furniture, signs and film installations, and
  whether a master must be engaged, is a question for the Federation of
  Icelandic Industries or a lawyer, not for this file.
