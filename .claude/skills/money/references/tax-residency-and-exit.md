## 9. What the facts allow [I]

- Leaving Iceland does not end Icelandic tax on its own. For up to three years
  the person must be able to prove he is taxed as a resident somewhere else. A
  country with no personal income tax fits the rule's tail, not its exception.
  How Skatturinn reads New Zealand's four-year exemption against the statute's
  words about being taxable like a resident there is an open question for the
  adviser.
- With no treaty between Iceland and New Zealand or the UAE, nothing caps
  Iceland's 22 % on dividends and on a sale of the Icelandic company's shares,
  and no tie-breaker settles a double residence.
- A foreign company owned and run by one person is resident, for tax, where
  that person sits: in Iceland while he lives there, in New Zealand once he
  lives there. Incorporating abroad moves paperwork, not tax, unless real
  management moves too.
- While he is resident in Iceland, a UAE company's profit is taxed to him in
  Iceland each year whether paid out or not.
- The UAE free-zone zero rate does not reach a business selling courses or
  services to private persons.
- New Zealand's exemption covers dividends from abroad but not income from
  personal services performed for a foreign company.
- Questions for the adviser: what proof Skatturinn accepts under art. 1; what
  Act 71/2026 changes; how the Latvia treaty treats gains on shares; whether
  the company should be sold, kept or wound up before a move.
