# Drone facade cleaning in Iceland — viability check, 2026-09-25

Trigger: Foxtech AeroClean T-M400C ad (tethered water + power kit for the
DJI Matrice 400). Question: setup cost, margin, competition, viability.

Method limits: cloud session; the network proxy blocked every page fetch
(foxtech, store.foxtech.com, foxtechrobotics, coptrz, ktvworkingdrone,
island.is, droneskycleaners). All facts below come from **search-result
snippets**, not from reading the pages. Anything not in a snippet is
labelled estimate or unverified.

## Verdict

**Not viable as "cleaning houses".** Possibly viable as a narrow B2B
service for tall or hard-to-reach surfaces, but only after it is proven
there is demand. Do not buy equipment yet.

Why:
1. Most of Iceland's building stock is low-rise. Ground crews with poles and
   pressure washers already do this cheaply. One Icelandic window-cleaning
   Facebook post lists **13,500 kr per apartment up to the 4th floor and
   15,500 kr for a terraced, semi-detached or detached house** (date
   unknown). A drone cannot compete with that price.
2. A drone only wins where the alternative is a bucket truck, a lift or
   scaffolding: high-rise glass, towers, silos, industrial tanks, large
   roofs. Iceland has few such targets, and KTV has already served the
   industrial part from Norway.
3. Weather sharply limits working days (see below).

## Regulation

- Iceland applies EU 2019/947 and 2019/945 (via regulation 1360/2024,
  according to a snippet).
- Commercial facade cleaning = **Specific category** → operational
  authorisation from Samgöngustofa after a SORA risk assessment (EASA and
  industry snippets). Flying over or near people in urban areas is the main
  ground-risk driver. Expect to cordon off the area under the drone.
- 150 m exclusion around hospitals, police stations, ministries, embassies
  and similar sites (snippet summary of island.is). Check the drone map for
  every job.

## Pricing and margin — scenario only

- Europe: drone cleaning quoted at **€3–6/m²** (drone-clean-up.ro snippet).
  At about 150 ISK/€ (assumption) that is ≈ 450–900 kr/m².
- Throughput: vendor claims (e.g. "1000 m²/h" for Foxtech P3) are headline
  figures. Allow for set-up, moving and weather: estimate 150–400 m²/h net.
- Working season: judgement. Spraying water needs above-freezing
  temperatures, and precise spraying needs wind well under the 12 m/s
  airframe limit (think ≤ 6–8 m/s). Realistically April–October, maybe
  **50–100 working days per year** in Reykjavík.
- Variable cost per job is low (water, fuel, a second person on the ground
  for safety and hose handling). Gross margin per job could be 60–75 %
  **if** jobs exist. The binding limit is volume, not margin.
- Break-even (scenario): about 10 m ISK capex over 4 years ≈ 2.5 m/yr plus
  insurance, vehicle, maintenance and the authorisation ≈ **4–5 m ISK/yr fixed**.
  At 600 kr/m² that is ~7,000–8,500 m² billed per year just to cover
  costs, before paying yourself. That is roughly 15–25 mid-size tall
  facades. Unproven that Iceland supplies that many per season.

## Competition in Iceland

- **No Icelandic drone-cleaning operator found** in Icelandic or English
  searches. I could not verify that none exists.
- **KTV Group / KTV Working Drone (Norway)** cleaned Samherji's land-based
  fish-farm tanks in Iceland (over 200 tanks planned). They have already
  taken the most obvious industrial niche, and they fly in.
- **Drone Sky Cleaners** advertises a *franchise* for Iceland (page blocked,
  terms unknown). This is an offer, not a local operator.
- Conventional competitors, established: Glersýn (own bucket trucks),
  Gluggaþvottur ehf (since 1998), Berglind (30+ years of pressure washing),
  Hreinsum, Sólar, Björt, AÞ Þrif, Gluggagæjarnir, BG Þjónustan.

## Technical risks specific to Iceland

- **Substrate:** 110–160 bar is fine for glass and metal cladding. On
  painted render or old paint it can strip paint and force water into
  cracks. Many Icelandic facades are painted concrete. Needs a soft-wash
  method and a sample patch before every new facade type.
- **Spotting on glass:** professional glass cleaning uses purified water.
  Reykjavík tap water is said to be soft (unverified). Test before relying
  on it.
- **Hose under wind load:** an 80 m hose hanging in gusts is a control and
  facade-strike risk. Judgement: this is the real operating limit, not the
  airframe's 12 m/s.
- **Winter:** −20 °C airframe rating is irrelevant; water freezes on the
  facade.

## Recommended next steps (cheap validation, ≈ 0 kr)

1. Ask Foxtech for a written quote for the T-M400C, with and without the
   M400, delivered to Iceland; ask for **real-world m²/h** and customer
   references in windy climates.
2. Call 8–10 buyers: large property companies (e.g. Reitir, Eik, Heimar),
   hotels and office towers, housing associations with 6+ floors, and
   industrial sites (fish farming, silos, power plants). Ask what they pay
   now and how often.
3. One call to Samgöngustofa: is there a PDRA/standard scenario that fits
   urban tethered cleaning, and how long does a first SORA take?
4. Alternative with no capital: offer KTV a local partner/subcontractor
   role instead of buying equipment.

Go only if steps 2–3 show **≥ 10,000 m² of committed or likely work per
season**.

Brand note: facade washing is not Preiss Workshop's positioning (interiors,
carpentry, CNC, signage). If pursued, run it as a separate name.
Height-access signage installs are the only natural overlap.
